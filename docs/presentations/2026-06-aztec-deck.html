Why this exists

Public order flow is a tax.

  • Every pending order on a transparent venue is a billboard — front-running, sandwiching and selective inclusion price that information against the trader.
  • Most "MEV protection" just relocates trust: a private relay sees your flow instead of the public, and you hope it behaves.
  • Two properties must hold simultaneously: pre-trade privacy (nobody can act on what they can't see) and verifiable execution (privacy can't become a cover for operator cheating).

Batch auctions give fairness. ZK gives verifiability. Aztec gives hiding. Quetzal is all three at once.

The protocol in one slide

Commit → reveal → clear → prove → settle.

01 · ON-CHAINCommitOrder enters the epoch as a Poseidon commitment folded into an accumulator. Side, size, price, owner — all hidden. Decoys obscure even the count.
02 · OFF-CHAINRevealPlaintext goes only to bonded aggregators discovered via an on-chain registry. The public still sees nothing.
03 · OFF-CHAINClearOne uniform price per pool per epoch — batch net demand crossed against concentrated AMM liquidity.
04 · ON-CHAINProve & settleUltraHonk proof verified on-chain before any funds move. Everyone in the epoch trades at the same price.

No resting book to front-run. No per-order execution to reorder. Nothing to sandwich.

Price discovery

Batch clearing over concentrated liquidity.

  • 16 geometric price buckets per pool above a floor price; each holds its own reserves, liquidity and per-share fee accumulators.
  • For a candidate price, the engine nets all eligible orders into one flow and walks the sqrt-price across bucket boundaries exactly as the contract executes it.
  • The bucket math exists three times — pool contract (Noir), clearing circuit (Noir), aggregator (TS) — with parity tests pinning all three to identical outputs.
  • A guarded search over a bounded price band converges to the uniform p*; orders that don't cross simply don't fill.

Multi-pool epochs settle all active pools in one transaction under one proof.

Privacy ≠ trust

The aggregator sees orders.
It cannot cheat with them.

  • Inclusion is forced: revealed orders must replay to the on-chain accumulator — drop, inject or mutate one and both the replay and the circuit fail.
  • Price is proven: fills and payouts come from circuit public inputs, not operator claims.
  • Conservation is independent: the contract re-checks that aggregate token flows equal the sum of per-bucket deltas the proof committed to.
Validated live — not in a sandbox

Adversarial acceptance on the testnet deployment: fabricated pool state → "pool state commitment mismatch" · wrong pool → "pool_id pair mismatch" · mismatched flows → "a_from_pool != sum reserve_a_sub". All three revert with their exact designed messages.

Part two

Running this on alpha-testnet:
a field report.

One 48-hour hardening cycle, as it actually happened: fee outage → keep-alive race → reveal anchor drift → fee starvation mid-settlement. Every incident produced a shipped fix and a lesson we think your team will want.

All of it is written up in docs/mainnet-lessons.md in the repo.
Field finding 01 · private-state sync

Reorgs stall note discovery —
and reveal metadata drifts.

  • Under testnet reorgs ("Pruning data after block N due to reorg") PXE note discovery stalled for hours.
  • Our SDK needed each order's anchor block (folded into the on-chain commitment) — unreadable notes forced a mined-block fallback that poisoned reveals, and an all-or-nothing accumulator replay then stranded entire epochs.
  • Our fix: bounded anchor-repair searched and accepted only when the on-chain accumulator confirms — self-healing with zero added trust. Worked live, twice.
Ask № 1

Expose the proven tx's anchor block (historical header) on the wallet SDK's send/prove result. Builders shouldn't have to re-read their own notes to learn what their own transaction anchored to.

Field finding 02 · fee dynamics

Fee volatility makes liveness expensive.

  • Base feePerL2Gas swung 2.5e13 → 4.7e13 within one hour; anything pinned to a static ceiling broke repeatedly.
  • Keeping an empty orderbook open (epoch keep-alive) cost ~4 FJ per close_epoch calm, ~27 FJ spiked — 280 to 2,300 FJ per day doing nothing.
  • getCurrentMinFees does an L1 read that 429s on the public RPC — every team will rediscover this the hard way.
Ask № 2

A stable, rate-limit-friendly fee oracle endpoint — and published guidance on fee-juice provisioning for infrastructure operators (the community faucet's ~3 drips/day/address is tight for a daemon that pays fees around the clock).

Field finding 03 · ops patterns that worked

Patterns we'd recommend to every Aztec team.

Pre-flight fee gatesCheck the fee payer's balance BEFORE destructive steps (queue drains). A broke wallet should delay work, not destroy it.
In-flight guardsKeep-alive vs settlement raced on our daemon — one epoch lost a fully-proven clear to its own auto-roll. One boolean fixed it; design for it up front.
External watchdogOur orderbook froze silently for ~17h while every process was "healthy". Alert on outcomes (epochs advancing) — silence is a failure mode.
Layered rate limitsApp-level per-IP + reverse-proxy per-route + global daily caps — each caught abuse the others missed.

All open source — fork the patterns, not just the protocol.

Consolidated

The builders' wish list.

  • SDK: anchor/historical-header exposure on send results · clearer note-sync status APIs (is discovery caught up? to which block?).
  • Fees: stable fee oracle · operator-scale fee-juice provisioning · docs on fee-reserve semantics (balance ≥ gasLimit × maxFee surprises everyone).
  • Network: a reorg/incident comms channel for testnet operators — we diagnosed reorgs from our own logs.
  • DX: TXE ⇄ live-testnet parity notes (what TXE can't catch — we hit several only in production).

We'll happily turn any of these into detailed issue reports with logs and repro timelines.
